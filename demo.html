Method
  • Tickets of a screening: Poisson count. Its mean θ: Gamma prior centred on the pattern.
  • k is a prior weight in screenings, not weeks: after k screenings of a film at that time, own data and pattern weigh the same.
  • Defaults c = 0.5, k = 4: tuned on mock histories (seeds 1-40), reported on seeds 41-80.

"Label any additional fields or operating rules as your own assumptions" (brief, §2). No additional record fields needed: the method runs on these six. Halls, seats, show times, runtimes and show limits are operating rules, marked assumed.

Decision required: confirm the six-field format is the fixed, complete export. Renamed or dropped fields, a changed meaning (e.g. purchase time instead of screening time) or pre-summed rows would be rejected or, worse, quietly degrade the estimates.
Gap: no real data. The brief specifies fields, not a dataset; size, period, quality and current practice are unknown. Every row here is generated from the settings.
  • Would predict better (beyond the six fields): other cinemas' sales of the same film, national box-office success, pre-sales, social-media reach and audience retention (trailer views, mentions), reviews, holidays, weather, competitor schedules, marketing.
  • Out of scope for step one: none is in the records, each needs its own data agreement, and their effect on one venue is unproven.
  • Simplification, distribution 1: demand per screening ~ negative binomial (mean = film strength x time-of-day shape x weekday factor, spread r). Why: buyers arrive as a count (Poisson) whose rate varies from screening to screening (Gamma), which is exactly the negative binomial (Lecture 2). The planner estimates r from the records; it never sees the generator's value.
  • Distribution 2: tickets per purchase ~ categorical 1-6 (30 / 45 / 10 / 10 / 3 / 2%). Sales = min(demand, seats), split into purchases.
  • The past schedule comes from a mock scheduler who knows roughly what sells (noise setting), so some film-time combinations are never tried, as in real records.

5 · Comparison

The baseline already optimises, but greedily

  • Ranks films by last week's tickets per show; gives each film shows in proportion, within its min / max.
  • Fills the best-selling show times first, biggest hall first, with the best-ranked film still available.
  • Each choice is the locally best one and is never revisited. It ignores that a film's demand depends on the time of day, and that a slot used now is gone for a better match later.
  • The optimised plan decides all cells of the week at once (linear program) on a per-film, per-time estimate.

6 · How it works

Methods from Lectures 0-4 only. Every figure below is drawn live from the current run.

Sum purchases into screenings

yi​=∑passes_bought over the rows of screening i. A total equal to a hall's seats is read as sold out assumed: demand was at least yi​. Used as recorded (cautious); an "at least the seats" correction did not improve plans.

Fit the pattern by maximum likelihood

μf,s,dpattern​=Ff​⋅Sg(f),s​⋅Dd​ Film strength x time-of-day index of the film's audience group (family / other, marked on the slate assumed) x weekday index. Poisson likelihood of all screening totals; each factor = observed / expected tickets over its rows, repeated until stable. Gives an estimate for every film-time-day, also never-shown ones. Lecture 2, MLE (p. 24).

Update each cell with Bayes' rule

Prior θ∼Gamma(kP, k): mean = pattern P, worth k screenings. Poisson likelihood of the cell's screenings (exposure wi​ = weekday weight). θ∣y∼Gamma(kP+∑i​yi​,  k+∑i​wi​),E[θ∣y]=k+∑i​wi​k​P+k+∑i​wi​∑i​wi​​yˉ​ A weighted average of pattern and own average. Lecture 2, Example 2.1 (p. 20); weighted average (pp. 16, 22); belief sharpening (p. 11).

Expected tickets in a given hall

Integrate, do not plug in. One screening's demand: mean μ=E[θ∣y]wd​, variance μ+μ2/r+wd2​Var(θ∣y) (Poisson count, screening spread r by maximum likelihood, estimation uncertainty), as a negative binomial with that mean and variance. vfhds​=E[min(Dfsd​, seatsh​)] A bigger hall is worth more only when demand can exceed the smaller one. Lecture 2, pp. 31-32; MLE p. 24.

Be cautious where the records are silent

Planning value = E[min(D′,seatsh​)], mean of D′ = μ(1−c⋅pattern share), pattern share =k/(k+∑wi​). Untried = all pattern = full cut c; well-observed = small cut. Only a new release (no records yet) is exempt and flagged: cutting it only shrinks a manual guess and starves new films of shows. A film that was available but never shown is cut like any untried cell. Worst case of a box around each value: still a linear program. Why: an optimiser seeks out the most optimistic extrapolations. Lecture 1, robust LP (p. 25), epigraph (p. 18); Lecture 0, p. 25; Lecture 1, p. 31.

Plan the week as a linear program

xfhds​∈{0,1}: film f in hall h, day d, slot s. max∑vfhds​xfhds​  s.t. f∑​xfhds​≤1,  h∑​xfhds​≤1,  minf​≤h,d,s∑​xfhds​≤maxf​,  xfhds​=0 if runtimef​+cleaning>slot Relaxed to x≥0. The optimum sits at a vertex (Lecture 1, p. 16); here every vertex is a whole schedule (hall rows and film rows form two nested families with 0/1 coefficients; a standard LP result, not on the slides). Solved by the simplex method; a fractional result would be refused.

Certify the plan

Weak duality: the dual prices give an upper bound on every feasible week. Bound = plan value, and every reduced cost ≤0, so no better week exists for the stated model (planning value, caution applied). Cost of a minimum-show rule: re-solved with the minimum one higher and one lower. Lecture 1, KKT and duality (pp. 37-42).

Scope, labels, what is real

Scope of step one

Must haveOne venue, one week, booked films; film x hall x show time; one film per hall-time; no film in two halls at once; min / max shows; runtime + cleaning fits the slot; demand learned from the six fields; comparison with a baseline.
Should haveCaution on untried combinations; optimality check; pins and blocks; reasons per cell; clear failure messages.
Could haveTest screenings of untried combinations (exploration); revenue objective once prices exist; holiday calendar.
Won't have nowFilm booking, pricing, several venues, mid-week changes, shows starting whenever a hall is free (flexible times), a film in two halls at once, after-midnight shows, staff rosters.

Labels

  • assumed = our own operating rule or reading, not in the brief; to be confirmed.
  • MOCK = scored against the generator's hidden demand; impossible with real records.
  • "untried" = a film never shown at that time and day type in the records; its number is the pattern.
  • "new release" = opens next week, no records; demand = a manual strength x the indices.
