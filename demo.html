Why this is an optimization

Nothing new is added. Only the arrangement changes, and the optimizer checks every arrangement allowed by the rules to find the best one.

How each plan is built

Both plans use the same films, halls, show limits and demand estimates. They differ only in how they decide.

Rule of thumb (baseline)
  1. Score each film by its tickets in the last history week (a film with no history gets the median score).
  2. Quota: shows = open screen-slots × the film's share of all scores, rounded and clipped to its min-max; leftover slots go to the top-scoring films still below their maximum.
  3. Fill slots in the order 19:00, 22:00, 16:00, 13:00, 10:00, biggest hall first, all 7 days.
  4. Pick the highest-scoring film that still has quota, fits the slot and is not already on in another hall at that time.

It never looks at demand per time slot, so a family film and a late-night blockbuster are treated alike. A reasonable reference, not the operator's known practice.

SLOTWISE optimizer

An integer program: choose x = 1 if film f plays in hall h on day d at slot s.

maximize   Σ v(f,h,d,s) · x(f,h,d,s)
subject to
  Σf x ≤ 1      for each hall, day, slot one film per hall
  minf ≤ Σh,d,s x ≤ maxf   for each film show limits
  Σh x ≤ 1      for each film, day, slot never two halls at once
  x = 0 if runtime + cleaning > slot length slot fit
  x ∈ {0, 1}
where v = E[min(demand, seats)]  expected tickets, demand lowered by caution × uncertainty

As a graph. Each unit of flow from S to T is one screening. It passes through a film, then "that film at that time" (capacity 1), then "that hall at that time" (capacity 1). Its value is the expected tickets on the last-but-one edge.

Every algorithm the tool runs, step by step

Why not try everything?

Each of the 105 screen-slots can hold one of 6 films or stay empty: about 7105 ≈ 1088 arrangements. Checking them one by one is impossible.

Why a network flow?

The rules form nested capacity limits, exactly the shape of a flow network. For such problems the best flow is automatically whole-numbered, so the exact best schedule is found in milliseconds (min-cost flow, successive shortest paths).

Why not a greedy rule?

Greedy filling commits slot by slot. The flow can reroute: it moves a film out of one slot when its limited shows are worth more elsewhere, which is how it finds trades like the accepted loss above.

How much to trust combinations nobody has tried

The records only show what the previous programmer chose to screen. For everything else the model extrapolates, and an optimizer is drawn to exactly those guesses. Here the plan is rebuilt for each caution level and scored against the hidden truth of this simulated world mock. In reality the truth is unknown, which is why caution is set by policy and checked in a trial.

How the numbers are calculated
  1. Data. 14k fictional purchase rows (the six assignment fields) are summed per screening. A schedule log (an information request) adds the hall, so sold-out screenings and screenings with zero sales become visible.
  2. Demand. For each film, time slot and weekday/weekend: estimate = (k · pattern + sum of tickets) / (k + screenings). The pattern is film × time × day type; with few screenings it dominates, with many the data does. Sold-out screenings count as "at least capacity".
  3. Value of a slot. expected tickets = E[min(demand, seats)], with demand lowered by the caution level × its uncertainty.
  4. Plan and baseline. See "How each plan is built" above.

Reproduce a result

The same inputs always give the same plans; the run fingerprint (next to the Run button) proves it. The data is regenerated from the seed. The file works offline, with no server or installation.
