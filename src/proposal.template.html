The problem

Same films, halls and hours: the arrangement decides how many seats fill.

The decision, every week

"Movie programming means choosing which movies to screen, in which screening rooms, and at what times" (brief). One venue, one week: 105 hall-times in the demo (3 halls x 5 shows x 7 days), 6-8 films.

A family film at 22:00, or a sell-out film in the smallest hall on Saturday evening, loses tickets the same films could have sold.

Current practice is unknown (brief): compared with a stated greedy rule, not with a real team.

Who uses it

  • The weekly scheduler: enters films and limits, reviews reasons, pins, approves.
  • The venue: halls, closures, show times, cleaning time.
  • The programming lead: KPI, thresholds, go / no-go.
  • The data owner: exports, data contract.

Roles assumed; confirmed in the interview.

Expected value

  • More admissions from the same films, halls and hours.
  • Fewer turned away at peak, fewer near-empty large halls.
  • A defensible plan: numbers and runner-up per cell.

Demo week (fictional): {{propTickets}} vs {{baseTickets}} expected tickets for the greedy baseline ({{gainPct}}) and {{repeatPromised}} for repeating last week ({{repeatGainPct}}), all on the tool's own estimate.

A property of invented data; a real uplift is measured in a trial. No money figure before that.

"Label any additional fields or operating rules as your own assumptions" (brief). No additional record fields needed; the tool runs on these six. Halls, seats, show times, cleaning time, runtimes and show limits are weekly operating settings, marked assumed until confirmed.

Decision required: confirm the six-field format as the fixed, complete export. Changed fields or meanings get rejected or, worse, quietly degrade the estimates.

Why the records cannot carry the full decision

  • No hall, no seats: a sell-out records the seats, not the demand.
  • Only purchases: a screening that sold nothing leaves no row.
  • Only what was scheduled: a film never shown at 22:00 has no evidence for 22:00.
  • No price, cost, purchase time, film data.

Hence a narrower first step (slide 5) and an information plan (slide 11).

Gap: no real dataset

Size, period, quality unspecified (brief). The demo generates {{rows}} purchase rows ({{historyFirst}} to {{historyLast}}) from its settings with two distributions: negative-binomial demand per screening (Poisson count with a rate varying by screening; the planner estimates the spread, r = {{dispersion}}) and categorical 1-6 tickets per purchase. Better prediction would need other cinemas' sales, box-office success, pre-sales, social-media reach and retention, reviews, holidays, weather: out of scope for step one (not in the records, effect on one venue unproven).

Scope

A narrower first step that still makes the real decision.

We use demand estimated from purchase records, hall seats and the confirmed film list, before next week's schedule is published, to decide which film plays in which hall at each show time, in order to increase expected admissions without breaking show commitments.

Must haveOne venue, one week (Mon-Sun), booked films; film x hall x show time; one film per hall-time; no film in two halls at once; min / max shows per film; runtime + cleaning time fits the slot; demand from the six fields; side-by-side with a baseline; advisory only (pin, block, re-run; nothing published automatically).
Should haveCaution on untried combinations; optimality certificate; reasons and runner-up per cell; clear messages when rules cannot be met; thin-evidence warning.

Won't have nowCommon in practice, left out of step one: see the list on the right, each with the reason.

Won't have now - out of scope

  • Film booking, run length, distributor terms: upstream; the film list is an input.
  • Pricing, dynamic pricing, concessions, ad revenue: no price or cost data.
  • Demand signals: social-media buzz and audience retention, reviews, search trends, national box office, pre-sales: not in the records.
  • Location: nearby cinemas, catchment and travel time, competitor schedules: one venue, no location data.
  • Calendar: weather, holidays, local events: not in the records.
  • Several venues; mid-week reprogramming: single venue; needs purchase times.
  • Flexible or staggered start times: the fixed grid keeps the evidence and the certificate; cost: idle hall minutes.
  • A film in two halls, after-midnight shows, premium formats, language versions, accessible screenings, event cinema, private hire.
  • Staff and cleaning rosters; personal offers via loyalty accounts (privacy).

Inputs, processing, outputs, workflow

From purchase rows to a week that can be defended.

InputsSix-field purchase rows; weekly settings: halls, seats, show times, cleaning time, films, runtimes, min / max shows.
1ScreeningsSum tickets per film, venue, date, time. Total = a hall size: flagged sold out.
2PatternFilm strength x time of day (family / other) x weekday, maximum likelihood.
3Bayes updatePer film, time, day type: pattern = prior, own screenings = evidence.
4ValueExpected tickets per hall with the fitted spread; pattern part cut (caution).

OutputsWeek grid, reasons and runner-up per cell, untried flags, cost of each rule, baseline next to it.

The calculation, every step

1 Screenings\(y_i = \sum \text{passes\_bought}\); read as sold out if \(y_i\) equals a hall's seatsassumed
2 Pattern\(\max \sum_i \big(y_i \log \mu_i - \mu_i\big)\), \(\mu_i = F_f\,S_{g(f),s}\,D_d\); each factor \(\leftarrow\) observed \(\div\) expectedL2 MLE
3 Bayes\(\theta \sim \text{Gamma}(kP, k) \Rightarrow \text{Gamma}\big(kP + \sum y,\ k + \sum w\big)\), mean \(\tfrac{k}{k+\sum w}P + \tfrac{\sum w}{k+\sum w}\bar y\), \(k = 4\)L2 Ex. 2.1
3b Spread\(r = \arg\max_r \sum_i \log \text{NB}\big(y_i \mid \hat\mu_{-i}, r\big)\) (leave-one-out cell means)L2 MLE
4 Value\(D \sim \text{NB}\), mean \(\mu\), variance \(\mu + \mu^2/r + w^2\,\text{Var}\,\theta\); \(v = \mathbb{E}\min(D, \text{seats}_h)\); planning mean \(\mu\big(1 - c\,\tfrac{k}{k+\sum w}\big)\), \(c = 0.5\), new releases exemptL2 p. 31; L1 robust LP
5 Week LP\(\max \sum v\,x\) s.t. \(\sum_f x \le 1\), \(\sum_h x \le 1\), \(\min_f \le \sum_{h,d,s} x \le \max_f\), \(x = 0\) if runtime + cleaning \(>\) slot, \(0 \le x \le 1\); optimum at a vertex = whole scheduleL1
5b Certificateplanning value \(=\) dual bound \(b^\top y\), all reduced costs \(\le 0\)L1 duality

Worked example: {{exFilm}}, {{exTime}}, {{exType}}: ({{exK}} x {{exPattern}} + {{exSumY}}) / ({{exK}} + {{exW}}) = {{exAlpha}} / {{exBeta}} = {{exPost}} tickets per screening ({{exN}} screenings, {{exWeight}} own evidence). The baseline uses only step 1 (last week's film averages) and a greedy fill.

The weekly workflow (proposed)

  1. Export last weeks' rows (six fields) for the venue.
  2. Confirm halls, closures, show times, cleaning time, films, runtimes, min / max shows.
  3. Run: draft next to the baseline, with flags for untried combinations and new releases.
  4. Inspect cells, pin or block, re-run; the plan stays optimal around the pins.
  5. Approve and publish as today; the tool never publishes.
  6. After the week, sales flow back into the records.

Every step runs in the demo ({{lpVars}} variables, {{lpRows}} rules, solved in the browser in well under a second).

The key insight

The records only show what was scheduled, and an optimiser hunts for whatever they leave out.

1 · Sell-outs cap the evidence

A full house records its seats, not its demand: {{soldOutRead}} of {{screenings}} demo screenings ({{soldOutPct}}). Plain averages understate the films that most need a bigger hall.

Response: flagged, used as recorded (cautious). Request: the screening log with halls.

2 · Empty screenings vanish

No purchase, no row: weak slots look better than they are.

Response: stated limitation; not repairable from purchases. Request: the same screening log (high priority).

3 · Untried combinations are guesses

Only {{cellsTested}} of {{cellsTotal}} film x time x day-type combinations were ever tried. The rest is extrapolation, and an optimiser picks exactly the most optimistic ones ("the optimiser will exploit the surrogate wherever it is wrong", Lecture 0).

Response: the pattern part of every estimate is cut (caution). MOCK The baseline's {{baseUntried}} untried screenings: estimated {{baseUntriedEst}} tickets, truly {{baseUntriedTrue}}.

What caution is worth MOCK

With a new release, no caution sells {{nrNoCautionAbs}} ± {{nrNoCautionCi}} fewer tickets a week; cutting the new film itself costs {{nrCautionNewAbs}} ± {{nrCautionNewCi}}, so it is exempt. Where combinations were untried only by chance, full caution loses {{spCaution1Abs}} ± {{spCaution1Ci}}; c = 0.5 is the balance. The sweep also showed that a film available but never scheduled must be cautioned, not exempt.

What it means for the trial

The backtest can only check combinations that were scheduled. So the trial includes a few flagged test screenings per week (exploration, Lecture 4), capped by agreement; their results calibrate the caution level.

Real-world evaluation plan

Three gated stages; each must earn the next.

Stage 0 · Data auditAPPROVAL REQUIRED

One venue. Confirm the data contract; measure sell-out share, zero-sale share (with the log), combination coverage, week-to-week variability.

Continue if hall seats and show times are confirmed and at least 8 recent weeks are usable proposed. Log strongly preferred; without it, continue with sell-outs read from totals. Stop at demand analytics if no hall information exists.

Owners: data owner (exports), analyst (report).

Stage 1 · Offline replayAPPROVAL REQUIRED

Rebuild past weeks. Backtest on held-out weeks; on screenings a replayed plan shares with what was shown, promised vs actual (over-promising?); operability judged by the scheduler.

Continue if error at least 10% below film averages, over-promising under 10%, 8 of 10 replayed weeks operable with minor edits proposed. Revise once if not; then stop.

Owners: analyst (replay), scheduler (operability).

Stage 2 · Advisory trialDECIDED LATER

Pairs of weeks at the same venue; a coin flip picks the tool-assisted week of each pair (randomised switchback); no holiday or opening weeks; capped flagged test screenings.

Adopt if tool weeks beat their paired own weeks on admissions per available seat by the margin agreed before the trial, interval above zero, no operational incidents. Length from Stage 0 variability (TBD).

Owners: programming lead (sign-off), scheduler (runs it).

ConditionWhen
TestStage 0 and 1 gates pass; KPI and thresholds signed off before the first trial week.

Metrics

  • Primary: admissions per available seat, tool week minus own week within each randomised pair.
  • Secondary: sell-outs and estimated turned-away demand, empty seats in large halls, test-screening results.
  • Model health: forecast error vs film averages; promised vs actual, weekly.
  • Adoption: share of cells overridden, planning time.

Separate from the demo's functional checks. proposed = our suggestion, to be agreed; trial margin and length TBD (depend on unseen variability).
