"Label any additional fields or operating rules as your own assumptions" (brief). We need no additional record fields; the tool runs on these six. Why the records are insufficient for the full decision

  • No hall, no seats: a sold-out show records the seats, not the demand.
  • Only purchases: a screening that sold nothing leaves no row at all.
  • Only what was scheduled: a film never shown at 22:00 has no evidence for 22:00.
  • No price, cost or purchase time: no revenue objective, no booking curves, no mid-week reaction.
  • No film data: runtimes, releases and new titles come from the manager.

Hence a narrower first step (E5) plus an information plan (E11).

Gap: there is no real dataset

Size, period, quality and current practice are unspecified (brief), so the demo generates {{rows}} purchase rows ({{historyFirst}} to {{historyLast}}) from your settings with two distributions: negative-binomial demand per screening (a Poisson count whose rate varies by screening; the planner estimates its spread from the records, r = {{dispersion}}) and a categorical 1-6 tickets per purchase. Accuracy would improve with real records and, beyond them, other cinemas' sales, national box-office success, pre-sales, social-media reach and retention, reviews, holidays and weather. Those are out of scope for step one: not in the records and unproven for one venue.

SLOTWISES1:E5FICTIONAL DATA
Scope

A narrower first step that still makes the real decision.

The programming manager uses demand estimated from purchase records, hall seats and the confirmed slate, before next week's schedule is published, to decide which film plays in which hall at each show-time, in order to increase expected admissions without breaking show commitments.

In scope

  • One venue, one week (Mon-Sun), a fixed show-time grid; decided once, before sales open.
  • Films already booked, with min / max shows per film (distributor and house rules, values TBD).
  • Rules: one film per hall-time; a film not in two halls at once (records could not tell them apart); runtime + changeover fits the slot.
  • Advisory: the manager can pin, block and re-run; nothing is published automatically.

Structure (course classification)

One decision, weekly-plan: Flat (booking is upstream and only an input), Stateless (no state links days inside the week; audience depletion is an assumption to check), Open-loop (the week is fixed before sales; a schedule must be published in advance). Objective and rules are Formulation components; the demand estimate is a supporting analysis that supplies their parameters.

Excluded, and what that costs

Film bookingWe cannot say which films to acquire; the slate is an input.
Pricing, concessions, revenueObjective is admissions; a ticket is not all margin (KPI to confirm).
Several venuesSister venues sharing an audience are ignored.
Mid-week changesNo reaction to a flop inside the week (needs purchase times; becomes a feedback problem).
Holidays, film ageing, weatherNot in the records; holiday weeks should be excluded from the trial.
A film in two halls at once, after-midnight showsNot supported in step one.

Inputs, processing, outputs, workflow

From purchase rows to a week the manager can defend.

InputsSix-field purchase rows; weekly settings: halls and seats, show-times, slate, runtimes, min / max shows, caution.
1ScreeningsSum tickets per film, venue, date, time. A total equal to a hall size is flagged as sold out.
2PatternFilm strength x time-of-day (family / other) x weekday, maximum likelihood.
3Bayes updatePer film, time, day type: pattern as prior, own screenings as evidence.
4ValueExpected tickets in each hall with the fitted spread; pattern part discounted (caution).
5Weekly LPBest week under the rules; certified by duality.
OutputsWeek grid, reasons and runner-up per cell, flags for untried bets, cost of each rule, baseline next to it.

The calculation

\(P_{fsd} = F_f\,S_{g(f),s}\,D_d\)   pattern by maximum likelihood, time index per audience group \(g\) (family films vs others) (Lecture 2)

\(\mathbb{E}[\theta\mid y] = \dfrac{k}{k+\sum w}\,P + \dfrac{\sum w}{k+\sum w}\,\bar y\)   Poisson-Gamma posterior mean: a weighted average of pattern and own average (\(\sum w\) = screenings weighted by weekday), \(k = 4\) (Lecture 2, Example 2.1)

\(v_{fhds} = \mathbb{E}[\min(D,\ \text{seats}_h)]\), \(D\) negative binomial with the screening spread \(r\) fitted by maximum likelihood plus estimation uncertainty ("integrate, don't plug in", Lecture 2); planning value \(\mathbb{E}[\min(D',\ \text{seats}_h)]\) with mean of \(D'\) = \(\mu\,(1 - c\cdot k/(k+\sum w))\), \(c = 0.5\); films without records exempt (robust LP, Lecture 1)

\(\max \sum v\,x\) s.t. one film per hall-time, no film in two halls at once, \(\min_f \le \text{shows}_f \le \max_f\), slot fit; LP optimum at a vertex (Lecture 1); here every vertex is a whole schedule (a standard LP result, not on the slides)

Worked example from the demo: {{exFilm}}, {{exTime}}, {{exType}}. Pattern P = {{exPattern}}; {{exN}} screenings sold {{exSumY}} tickets with exposure {{exW}} (own average {{exOwn}}). With k = {{exK}}: ({{exK}} x {{exPattern}} + {{exSumY}}) / ({{exK}} + {{exW}}) = {{exAlpha}} / {{exBeta}} = {{exPost}} tickets per screening, {{exWeight}} from its own evidence. The demo's demand map shows the same numbers for that cell.

The weekly workflow (proposed)

  1. Data owner exports last weeks' rows (six fields) for the venue.
  2. Manager confirms halls, closures, show-times, slate, runtimes and min / max shows.
  3. Run: the draft appears next to the rule of thumb, with flags for untried combinations and for films without records.
  4. Manager inspects cells, pins or blocks, re-runs; the plan stays optimal around the pins.
  5. Manager approves and publishes as today; the tool never publishes.
  6. After the week, sales flow back into the records for the next run.

All of this runs in the demo ({{lpVars}} variables, {{lpRows}} rules, solved in the browser in well under a second).

SLOTWISES1:E9FICTIONAL DATA
The key insight

The records only show what was scheduled, and an optimiser hunts for whatever they leave out.

1 · Sell-outs cap the evidence

A full house records its seats, not its demand. In the demo history {{soldOutRead}} of {{screenings}} screenings ({{soldOutPct}}) are read as sold out. Plain averages therefore understate the films that most need a bigger hall.

Response: sold-out totals are flagged and used as recorded, so estimates there stay cautious; an "at least the seats" correction did not improve plans in the mock study. Request: the screening log with halls makes this exact.

2 · Empty screenings vanish

A screening with no purchase leaves no row, so weak slots look better than they are and the record of what was shown is incomplete.

Response: flagged as a limitation; it cannot be repaired from purchases alone. Request: the same screening log (high priority).

3 · Untried combinations are guesses

Only {{cellsTested}} of {{cellsTotal}} film x time x day-type combinations were ever tried. For the rest the estimate is an extrapolation, and an optimiser picks exactly the most optimistic ones ("the optimiser will exploit the surrogate wherever it is wrong", Lecture 0).

Response: the pattern part of every estimate is valued at the low end (mean x (1 - c x pattern share)), so untried combinations count least; a new release is planned on the manager's estimate and flagged. MOCK The rule of thumb's {{baseUntried}} untried screenings are estimated at {{baseUntriedEst}} tickets but would truly sell {{baseUntriedTrue}}.

What caution is worth MOCK

With a new release in the slate, a plan without caution sells {{nrNoCautionAbs}} ± {{nrNoCautionCi}} fewer tickets a week; applying the caution to the new film itself would cost {{nrCautionNewAbs}} ± {{nrCautionNewCi}}, so it is exempt. Where combinations were untried only by chance, full caution would lose {{spCaution1Abs}} ± {{spCaution1Ci}}; c = 0.5 is the balance.

What it means for the trial

The backtest can only check combinations that were scheduled. So the trial deliberately includes a few flagged test screenings per week (exploration, Lecture 4) to learn what the records cannot show, with a cap agreed with the manager; their results calibrate the caution level.

SLOTWISES1:E10FICTIONAL DATA
Real-world evaluation plan

Three gated stages; each one must earn the next.

Stage 0 · Data auditASK NOW

One venue. Confirm the data contract; measure sell-out share, zero-sale share (with the log), combination coverage and week-to-week variability of admissions.

Continue if hall seats and the show-time grid are confirmed and at least 8 recent weeks are usable [PROPOSED]. The screening log is strongly preferred; without it we continue with sell-outs flagged from totals and the zero-sale bias stated. Stop at demand analytics if no hall information exists.

Stage 1 · Offline replayASK NOW

Rebuild past weeks. Backtest the estimate on held-out weeks; on the screenings a replayed plan shares with what was shown, compare promised with actual tickets (does the tool over-promise?); the manager judges operability.

Continue if the error is at least 10% below the film-average error, over-promising stays under 10%, and at least 8 of 10 replayed weeks are operable with minor edits [PROPOSED]. Revise once if not; then stop.

Stage 2 · Advisory trialDECIDE LATER

Pairs of weeks at the same venue; a coin flip decides which week of each pair uses the tool-assisted plan (randomised switchback), excluding holiday and opening weeks; a capped number of flagged test screenings.

Adopt if tool weeks beat their paired own weeks on admissions per available seat by the margin agreed before the trial, with the interval above zero, and no operational incidents. Length: from the variability measured in Stage 0 (TBD).

ConditionWhen
TestStage 0 and Stage 1 gates pass; KPI and thresholds signed off by the sponsor before the first trial week.
AdoptTrial margin met; manager override rate falls below an agreed level; data export stable for the trial.
ReviseFrequent overrides (a missing rule, e.g. staffing or distributor terms); flagged test screenings underperform (raise caution); over-promising grows; estimate drifts (holidays, ageing).
StopNo halls or seats obtainable; estimate not better than averages after one revision; trial shows no gain or a loss; the data contract keeps changing.

Metrics

  • Primary: admissions per available seat, tool week minus own week within each randomised pair (adjusted for the slate's national performance if available).
  • Secondary: sell-outs and estimated turned-away demand, empty seats in large halls, results of flagged test screenings.
  • Model health: forecast error against film averages and promised-vs-actual tickets, every week.
  • Adoption: share of cells overridden, planning time.

These real-world criteria are separate from the demo's functional checks. Thresholds marked [PROPOSED] are our suggestion to be agreed with the sponsor; the trial margin and length are TBD because they depend on variability we have not seen.
